# Food Data Sources

## Purpose

This document explains how nutritional information for food items should be collected, recorded, and evaluated for the DiaPlate project.

## Information to Record

For each food item, the following information should be documented:

- Food name
- Serving size
- Calories
- Carbohydrates
- Fibre
- Protein
- Sugar
- Sodium
- Glycemic Index, if available
- Data source
- Date accessed
- Whether the information is estimated or verified

## Possible Data Sources

### 1. Indian Food Composition Tables

The Indian Food Composition Tables provide information about the nutritional composition of Indian foods.

They can be investigated as a source for Indian ingredients and food items.

Publisher:

Indian Council of Medical Research – National Institute of Nutrition

### 2. USDA FoodData Central

USDA FoodData Central provides nutrition information for various foods and ingredients.

Website:

https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/

### 3. Food Packaging Labels

For packaged foods, nutrition labels can provide information such as:
- Calories
- Carbohydrates
- Sugar
- Fibre
- Protein
- Sodium

### 4. Research Papers

Peer-reviewed studies may provide information about the Glycemic Index of specific foods.

However, values may differ depending on food preparation and testing conditions.

## Data Quality Considerations

- Serving sizes must be consistent.
- Indian recipes can vary between households.
- Cooking methods can affect nutritional values.
- GI values may not be available for mixed dishes.
- Different sources may report different values.
- Unknown values should not be invented.

## Important Note

Estimated nutritional values must not be presented as clinically verified information.

The food database should be reviewed and updated when reliable information becomes available.

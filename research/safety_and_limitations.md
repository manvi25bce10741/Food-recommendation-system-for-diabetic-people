# Safety and Limitations

## Project Safety Statement

DiaPlate is an educational software prototype.

It is not intended to:
- Diagnose diabetes
- Prescribe treatment
- Calculate insulin doses
- Recommend medication changes
- Replace professional medical advice

## Main Limitations

### 1. Food Data

Nutritional values may vary depending on:
- Ingredients
- Serving size
- Cooking method
- Food brand
- Recipe

### 2. Individual Differences

People can respond differently to the same food.

Blood glucose response may depend on:
- Medication
- Physical activity
- Health status
- Portion size
- Individual physiology

### 3. Glycemic Index

GI values may not be available for all foods, especially mixed dishes.

GI alone cannot fully describe the effect of a complete meal.

### 4. Recommendation Scores

The scoring system is a prototype and has not been clinically validated.

The scores should not be interpreted as medical suitability ratings.

### 5. Medical Conditions

People with specific medical needs may require individualized guidance from qualified healthcare professionals.

Examples include:
- Type 1 diabetes
- Gestational diabetes
- Kidney disease
- Food allergies
- Complex medication plans

## Privacy Considerations

If the application collects personal or health-related information in the future, the team should:

- Collect only necessary information.
- Explain why information is collected.
- Avoid unnecessary storage of sensitive data.
- Use appropriate security measures.
- Provide a clear privacy notice.
- Consider applicable data protection requirements.

## Future Improvements

Before considering real-world health use, the project would require:

1. Expert review.
2. Verification of nutritional data.
3. Usability testing.
4. Safety testing.
5. Accessibility testing.
6. Bias evaluation.
7. Clinical and regulatory assessment where applicable.

## Conclusion

DiaPlate is intended to demonstrate how nutritional information and rule-based programming can be combined to provide educational meal suggestions.

It should not be used as a replacement for individualized healthcare guidance.
